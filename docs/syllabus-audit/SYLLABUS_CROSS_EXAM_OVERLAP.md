## Findings

### Common foundational core

The strongest overlap is in mathematical methods, classical mechanics, electrodynamics, quantum mechanics, thermodynamics/statistical physics, atomic/molecular physics, solid-state physics, nuclear/particle physics and electronics. These subjects are genuinely shared across the four examinations, but the depth and wording are not interchangeable.

### Exam-specific and depth-specific areas

- **CSIR Part A vs Part B:** CSIR is the only supplied reference with explicit Core and Advanced levels. Part B contains Green functions, PDEs, computational methods, tensors, SU(2)/O(3), Hamilton-Jacobi, relativistic quantum mechanics, advanced statistical physics and the advanced atomic/condensed/nuclear areas. Do not flatten Part A and Part B.
- **CSIR advanced-only topics:** plasma dispersion, transmission lines/waveguides, radiation from moving charges, WKB, relativistic quantum mechanics, phase transitions, BEC, magnetic ordering, liquid crystals and quasicrystals, and detailed nuclear/particle symmetries are explicit in Part B. Some are also in GATE at a different depth.
- **GATE 2025:** the supplied reference contains nine sections and is notably stronger than JEST in solid-state, atomic/molecular, nuclear/particle and electronics detail. It has no separate measurement/error-analysis section and no separate optical-physics section in the supplied 2025 document.
- **JEST:** the supplied pages give a compact six-heading syllabus. It is broad in optics, atomic/molecular, condensed-matter and nuclear/particle physics; many advanced topics are not specified. Its six headings, not an informal five-area description, are authoritative for this document.
- **TIFR:** only eight broad areas are given. The document explicitly describes the standard as undergraduate/master's-level Physics, so detailed subtopics are **inferred study coverage**, not official TIFR requirements. TIFR cannot justify “complete topic-by-topic” claims.

### Shared-hierarchy guidance

- Represent a shared academic concept once in a shared concept catalogue, then map it separately to each examination node and preserve exam-specific wording/depth.
- Do not merge CSIR Part B advanced topics into Part A, and do not merge GATE 2025 sections merely because JEST uses a shorter heading.
- Keep TIFR at broad-area level unless a verified detailed TIFR document is supplied. Inferred TIFR study notes may exist, but must be labelled as inferred.
- Use exam-specific node IDs for moves/splits. A shared concept ID is not a substitute for a stable syllabus node ID.

### TIFR limitation

The supplied TIFR document is a two-page sample-question paper. Page 1 lists the eight broad areas; page 2 contains sample questions. No page supplies a detailed topic list. Therefore, this audit can confirm the eight broad areas but cannot identify official TIFR subtopic gaps.
